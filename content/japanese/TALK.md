# Talk mode (Japanese): how adults learn to speak, and how the 80 dialogs use it

## Research used
1. **Anticipation and graduated-interval recall (Pimsleur).** The learner hears an English prompt and must produce the Japanese
   before hearing it: challenge and response, not repeat-after-me. Items come back at growing intervals, from seconds to days
   (Pimsleur 1967: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pimsleur_Language_Programs ;
   https://www.pimsleur.com/blog/why-graduated-interval-recall-is-the-key-to-mastering-a-new-language/).
   Talk mode does the same: Learn (hear it twice, repeat), then the Dialog (cue → answer from memory → the model line).
2. **Retrieval practice.** Recalling something beats studying it again, especially when the recalls are spaced out
   (Karpicke & Roediger 2008, https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1152408).
3. **Output practice (Swain's output hypothesis).** Speaking shows you the gap between what you want to say and what you
   can say, lets you test your guesses, and turns knowledge into fluent speech (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensible_output).
   From level 11 the cues give a goal ("Ask which platform"), not a translation, so the learner builds the sentence.
4. **High-frequency survival phrases and set expressions first.** すみません (sorry / excuse me / calling staff), お願いします,
   ください, いくらですか, 大丈夫です (also a polite "no thanks"), いただきます / ごちそうさまでした, どうも, よろしくお願いします
   (Paul Nation's survival Japanese list: https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/lals/resources/paul-nations-resources/vocabulary-lists/survival-vocabulary-lists/survival-japanese.pdf ;
   https://www.fluentu.com/blog/japanese/japanese-survival-phrases/). These are memorised whole as chunks and then reused inside longer sentences.
5. **Politeness and grammar order.** Adults speak to strangers in the polite です/ます form, so it is used everywhere except
   levels 38, 64 and 78 (casual talk with a close friend, flagged in the scene). Grammar follows JLPT N5 → N4 → N3, as in the
   lesson units: です/か → ください/ます → ている, たい, てもいいですか → と思います, ほうがいい, ませんか/ましょう, ておく →
   たら, ば, たことがある, てしまう, てくれる → そうです (hearsay), ようです, かもしれません, ので/のに, ないといけません,
   potential and passive, 〜ようになる (https://jlptsensei.com/jlpt-n4-grammar-list/ ; https://jlptsensei.com/jlpt-n3-grammar-list/).
6. **Keigo where adults actually meet it: work and the telephone.** From level 41 the learner hears and then says the fixed
   business openers — お世話になっております, ブラウンと申します, 承知しました / かしこまりました, 少々お待ちください,
   申し訳ありません, 折り返しお電話をお願いできますか, 失礼いたします — because set phrases carry politeness that grammar rules
   cannot teach quickly.
7. **あいづち and hedging carry a B1 conversation.** そうですね, 確かに, なるほど, おっしゃるとおりです keep the other person
   talking; ちょっと……, 〜かもしれません, 正直, どうも 〜ようです let the learner disagree, refuse and complain without rudeness.
8. **Listening stretches ahead of speaking.** The other person's lines are a little harder than yours. In levels 1–15 the narrator
   translates them. Staff and business Japanese (いらっしゃいませ, お調べいたします, ご記入ください) is heard long before the learner says it.

## The 16 themes (5 levels each; the length of your lines climbs from ~4 to ~28 characters)
1. **Greetings and introductions (1–5):** こんにちは, はじめまして, おはようございます, すみません, ありがとうございます, where you're from, good night.
2. **Café and restaurant (6–10):** ordering (ください / で), a table for two, いただきます, the bill, paying by card, coffee with a neighbour.
3. **Numbers, prices and shopping (11–15):** いくら, counters (つ, 枚), yen, a market stall, tickets, trying clothes on, a convenience store, souvenirs.
4. **Directions and transport (16–20):** asking the way and repeating it back, train tickets, platforms, a taxi, buses, getting lost in a station.
5. **Hotel, travel and fixing problems (21–25):** checking in, a broken air conditioner, a lost wallet at the kōban, a missed train, checking out.
6. **Small talk (26–30):** family, work, weekend hobbies, the weather, and a longer chat with a neighbour about life in Japan.
7. **Plans, invitations and time (31–35):** inviting (ませんか), agreeing when and where to meet, changing a plan, booking a restaurant, planning a day trip, phoning to say you're late.
8. **Experiences, health and opinions (36–40):** たことがある, at the doctor's, casual catching up with a friend (38), comparing Tokyo and Toronto, telling the story of your first day.
9. **Work and study (41–45):** your job and studies, a business phone call, a short team meeting, asking for and giving instructions, apologising for a mistake at work.
10. **On the phone and online (46–50):** phoning a support line, being put on hold and leaving a message, a parcel that never came, booking by phone, a bad line ("I only caught half of that").
11. **Telling stories (51–55):** yesterday step by step, a story with a surprise, a near miss, trouble on a trip, passing on what someone else told you (そうです / と言っていました).
12. **Opinions and disagreeing politely (56–60):** a film, agreeing (確かに, おっしゃるとおり), disagreeing with ちょっと…かもしれません, giving reasons (からです), changing your mind.
13. **Feelings and helping people (61–65):** worry, relief, congratulations and sympathy, comforting a friend (casual, 64), asking a favour and offering help.
14. **Everyday admin and problems (66–70):** the bank, a form and its questions, a complaint that gets resolved, cancelling a membership, explaining a misunderstanding.
15. **Culture, food and life here (71–75):** explaining Christmas in Canada, asking what is rude, learning to cook nikujaga, New Year in Japan, what surprised you here.
16. **Longer conversation (76–80):** plans and dreams, viewing a flat, meeting an old friend after a year (casual, 78), a three-way discussion, and the story of how you learned Japanese.

Recurring cast: Sam (Brown) from Canada; Ms Tanaka next door; Mr Sato and the team leader Ms Mori at work; Yuki and Kenta, friends.
Recycling: short set phrases come back as whole lines in two or three levels (no line is used in more than three), and as chunks
inside longer lines (すみません、…; …お願いします; 大丈夫です; …ですね; お世話になっております). Each level brings in only a few new patterns.
