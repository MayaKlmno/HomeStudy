# How adults learn French — and how this course follows it

## What the research says

1. **Learn the most frequent words first.** A small core of words covers most of any text: learners
   need roughly 95% coverage for adequate comprehension and 98% for comfortable reading
   ([Nation 2006](https://www.lextutor.ca/cover/papers/nation_2006.pdf);
   [Laufer & Ravenhorst-Kalovski 2010](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ887873.pdf)).
   For French, Lonsdale & Le Bras’s *Frequency Dictionary of French* (23-million-word corpus,
   5,000 entries) is the standard ranking
   ([Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/A-Frequency-Dictionary-of-French-Core-Vocabulary-for-Learners-1st-Edition/Lonsdale-Le-Bras/p/book/9780203883044)).
   Its top few hundred items are function words, être/avoir/faire/aller/pouvoir/vouloir/devoir/dire/voir,
   and everyday nouns (homme, femme, jour, temps, main, maison, vie, monde…).
2. **Grammar in a CEFR order.** A1: present of être/avoir and -er verbs, articles and gender,
   negation, questions, aller + infinitive. A2: passé composé, imparfait, reflexives, object
   pronouns, partitives. B1: futur simple, conditionnel, plus-que-parfait, si-clauses, first
   subjunctive ([Council of Europe CEFR](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages);
   [Kwiziq tenses by CEFR level](https://french.kwiziq.com/french-tenses-by-cefr-level)).
3. **Retrieval beats re-reading, and spacing beats cramming.** Testing yourself produces far better
   retention than restudying ([Karpicke & Roediger 2008, *Science*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18276894/)),
   and spreading practice over time improves long-term recall
   ([Cepeda et al. 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/)).
4. **Comprehensible input.** Acquisition grows from lots of meaningful input just above the
   learner’s level ([Krashen 1982](http://www.sdkrashen.com/content/books/principles_and_practice.pdf));
   authentic text works when most of its words are already known and the rest are glossed.

## How this curriculum applies it

- **Frequency first.** About 430 taught items across 90 teaching levels, each used once only. Units 1–5
  are almost entirely top-frequency vocabulary (greetings, family, être/avoir/faire/aller/venir,
  vouloir/pouvoir/devoir, numbers, time, food, money, the city). Book-specific words (cigale,
  chandelier, collier) are limited to a handful per unit and always sit next to common words.
  Checked across the track: no word is taught twice (articles ignored).
- **One new grammar point per level, in CEFR order:**
  - U1 Perrault (A1): gender and articles, être, avoir, c’est / il y a, plurals, question words, ne… pas.
  - U2 La Fontaine (A1): present of -er verbs by person, adjective agreement and position, on,
    est-ce que, manger/commencer spellings, toujours, meilleur.
  - U3 Verne (A1): numbers, clock time, days, aller, au/à la/aux, aller + infinitive, venir (de).
  - U4 Dumas (A1–A2): possessives, -ir verbs, faire, vouloir/pouvoir/devoir, sortir/dormir, tout,
    the imperative.
  - U5 Hugo (A2): partitives, quantities, prendre/boire, rien/personne/plus/jamais, pas de,
    le/la/les as pronouns, acheter/payer.
  - U6 Flaubert (A2): home, reflexive verbs, feelings, du/des after de, comparisons.
  - U7 Maupassant (A2): passé composé with avoir and être, irregular participles, agreement,
    negatives, reflexives in the past, déjà / pas encore; recognising the passé simple.
  - U8 Verne (A2–B1): imparfait, il y avait, imparfait vs passé composé, qui/que/où, si… que, y/en.
  - U9 Molière (B1): politeness, futur simple (regular and irregular), il faut, question words,
    savoir vs connaître, lui/leur, ne… que / ni… ni.
  - U10 Proust (B1): conditionnel, si-clauses, ce/cette/ces, celui/celle, plus-que-parfait,
    gerund, a first subjunctive.
- **Retrieval and spacing.** Each level asks the learner to produce: translate words, rebuild two
  sentences, pick the right form in a blank. Every level has new content, so review comes from
  the app’s spaced revisiting of past levels, not from recycling the same 12 sentences.
- **Comprehensible input from real books.** Each teaching level ends on a short verbatim line from
  its unit’s book that uses that level’s point (e.g. ne… plus in « vous n’appartenez plus au mal »,
  the future in « Je vous expliquerai à fond… »). Early units use very short lines; each unit
  closes with a 2–5-sentence reading passage, glossed, with comprehension questions.
