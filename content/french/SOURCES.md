# Sources for the French course

Every unit of the French track is anchored to a work of French literature that is in the public
domain. Vocabulary, practice sentences and blanks were written for this course. Each of the 90
teaching levels ends on a short verbatim quote from its unit’s book, and each unit closes with a
longer verbatim reading passage. Every quote was checked word for word against the edition listed
below (tools/check-content.js with a sources map), and chapter labels come from that edition’s
headings.

| Unit | Levels | Work | Author | First published | Edition used for quotes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–10 | *Contes* (Histoires ou contes du temps passé) | Charles Perrault (1628–1703) | 1697 | *Les Contes de Perrault*, ed. Pierre Féron, Casterman, 1902 — [Wikisource](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Contes_de_Perrault_(%C3%A9d._1902)) |
| 2 | 11–20 | *Fables* | Jean de La Fontaine (1621–1695) | 1668 | *Fables*, Bernardin-Béchet, 1874 — [Wikisource](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Fables_de_La_Fontaine_(%C3%A9d._1874)) (15 fables downloaded individually) |
| 3 | 21–30 | *Le Tour du monde en quatre-vingts jours* | Jules Verne (1828–1905) | 1872 | Project Gutenberg [#800](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/800) (ebooksgratuits text) |
| 4 | 31–40 | *Les Trois Mousquetaires* | Alexandre Dumas (1802–1870) | 1844 | Project Gutenberg [#13951](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/13951) |
| 5 | 41–50 | *Les Misérables* | Victor Hugo (1802–1885) | 1862 | Project Gutenberg, tomes I–V ([#17489](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/17489)–[#17519](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/17519), ebooksgratuits text) |
| 6 | 51–60 | *Madame Bovary* | Gustave Flaubert (1821–1880) | 1857 | Project Gutenberg [#14155](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/14155) |
| 7 | 61–70 | *Contes du jour et de la nuit* (incl. « La Parure ») | Guy de Maupassant (1850–1893) | 1885 | Marpon & Flammarion edition, Project Gutenberg [#14790](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/14790) |
| 8 | 71–80 | *Vingt mille lieues sous les mers* | Jules Verne (1828–1905) | 1870 | Hetzel illustrated edition, Project Gutenberg [#5097](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/5097) |
| 9 | 81–90 | *Le Bourgeois gentilhomme* | Molière (1622–1673) | 1670 | *Œuvres complètes*, Garnier, 1904, t. 3 — [Wikisource](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Le_Bourgeois_gentilhomme/%C3%89dition_Garnier,_1904) |
| 10 | 91–100 | *Du côté de chez Swann* | Marcel Proust (1871–1922) | 1913 | Project Gutenberg [#2650](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2650) |

Notes on labels: Maupassant quotes use the collection *Contes du jour et de la nuit* as the book
and the story title (« La Parure », « La Confession », « Adieu », « Un Parricide ») as the chapter.
Molière quotes are labelled « Acte II, scène 4 » etc. La Fontaine quotes give book and fable number
as printed in the 1874 edition (e.g. « Livre I, fable X : Le Loup et l’Agneau »).

Spelling follows each edition: the 1902 Perrault reads « la plus éveillée qu’on eût su voir »
(some later editions print « la plus jolie »), and the Gutenberg texts of Dumas, Hugo and
Maupassant sometimes spell « coeur » without the ligature.

All ten authors died more than 70 years ago and all ten works were published well over a century
ago, so the original French texts are in the public domain worldwide. The editions used are
public-domain printings (the newest is 1904) transcribed by Project Gutenberg and Wikisource.

## Piano melodies

The pieces in Piano unit 10 are traditional or public-domain melodies: *Au clair de la lune*,
*Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star*, *Frère Jacques*, *Ode to Joy* (Beethoven, 1824), *Jingle Bells*
(Pierpont, 1857), *Amazing Grace*, *Für Elise* (Beethoven, 1810), *Minuet in G* (Petzold, c. 1725),
*Greensleeves* (traditional, 16th c.) and *Eine kleine Nachtmusik* (Mozart, 1787).
