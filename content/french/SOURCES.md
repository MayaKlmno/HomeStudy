# Sources for the French course

Every unit of the French track is anchored to a work of French literature that is in the public
domain. Vocabulary and practice sentences are written for this course in the spirit of each work;
the passage that closes each unit is a short authentic excerpt, quoted with attribution.

| Unit | Levels | Work | Author | First published |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1–10 | *Contes* — « Le Petit Chaperon rouge » | Charles Perrault (1628–1703) | 1697 |
| 2 | 11–20 | *Fables* — « Le Corbeau et le Renard » | Jean de La Fontaine (1621–1695) | 1668 |
| 3 | 21–30 | *Le Tour du monde en quatre-vingts jours* | Jules Verne (1828–1905) | 1872 |
| 4 | 31–40 | *Les Trois Mousquetaires* | Alexandre Dumas (1802–1870) | 1844 |
| 5 | 41–50 | *Les Misérables* | Victor Hugo (1802–1885) | 1862 |
| 6 | 51–60 | *Madame Bovary* | Gustave Flaubert (1821–1880) | 1857 |
| 7 | 61–70 | « La Parure », *Contes et nouvelles* | Guy de Maupassant (1850–1893) | 1884 |
| 8 | 71–80 | *Vingt mille lieues sous les mers* | Jules Verne (1828–1905) | 1870 |
| 9 | 81–90 | *Le Bourgeois gentilhomme* | Molière (1622–1673) | 1670 |
| 10 | 91–100 | *Du côté de chez Swann* | Marcel Proust (1871–1922) | 1913 |

All ten authors died more than 70 years ago and all ten works were published well over a century
ago, so the original French texts are in the public domain worldwide. Full texts are available from
Project Gutenberg (gutenberg.org) and Wikisource (fr.wikisource.org).

## Piano melodies

The pieces in Piano unit 10 are traditional or public-domain melodies: *Au clair de la lune*,
*Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star*, *Frère Jacques*, *Ode to Joy* (Beethoven, 1824), *Jingle Bells*
(Pierpont, 1857), *Amazing Grace*, *Für Elise* (Beethoven, 1810), *Minuet in G* (Petzold, c. 1725),
*Greensleeves* (traditional, 16th c.) and *Eine kleine Nachtmusik* (Mozart, 1787).
