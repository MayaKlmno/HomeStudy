# Sources for the Spanish course

Every unit of the Spanish track is anchored to one work of Spanish-language literature that is in
the public domain. Vocabulary, practice sentences, blanks and explanations were written for this
course; every `quote.t` and `passage.t` is copied word for word from the edition listed below and
verified with `node tools/check-content.js spanish --sources …/sources.json`. Chapter labels come
from the headings of the downloaded text, not from memory.

## Public-domain status

All ten authors died more than 80 years ago (the longest term used in the Spanish-speaking world
for these authors: Spain gives life + 80 years to authors who died before 7 December 1987), so the
works are in the public domain in their countries of origin. Quiroga died in 1937 and Machado in
1939, the most recent of the ten. In the United States every edition used here was published before
1930, so it is public domain there as well; Project Gutenberg and Spanish Wikisource distribute the
exact files used.

## Notes on labels and spelling

* Bécquer: the book value is *Rimas y leyendas*; the edition used is the 1912 *Obras escogidas*,
  which prints both. Chapters are given as “Rimas, XXI” or by legend title (“Los ojos verdes”).
* Machado: the 1917 *Poesías completas* numbers its poems I–CXXXVIII and does not print a separate
  “Campos de Castilla” title page, so the book value is *Poesías completas* and chapter labels give
  the section and number as printed (“Soledades, XI”, “CXXXVI, Proverbios y cantares, XXVIII”).
  “Caminante, no hay camino…” is poem XXVIII of *Proverbios y cantares* in this edition (XXIX in
  many later ones).
* *La Regenta*: labels are “Tomo I, I” … “Tomo II, XXVII”, following the volume and chapter
  headings of the text.
* Calderón: labels are “Jornada primera, escena II” etc., as the 1881 *Teatro selecto* prints them.
* *Don Quijote*: labels say which book (“Primera parte (1605)” = the 1605 novel, “Segunda parte
  (1615)”) and then the chapter, because this file also contains the 1605 novel’s own internal
  “primera–cuarta parte” divisions.
* Old spelling is kept exactly as printed: Bécquer and Calderón write *á*, *ó*, *ilusion*,
  *ficcion*, *ménos*; Darío writes *fué*, *dió*, *dí*; Cervantes writes *mesmo*, *lantejas*.
  Glosses point out the modern forms.
